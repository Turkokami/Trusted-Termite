## 1. LICENSING STRUCTURE — 4 TAC Ch. 7, Subchapter H

Chapter 7 is **Pesticides**; Subchapter H is **Structural Pest Control Service**, organized into 7 divisions:

1. General Provisions · 2. Licenses · 3. Compliance and Enforcement · 4. Unlawful Acts and Grounds for Revocation · 5. Treatment Standards · 6. Structural Pest Control Advisory Committee · 7. Integrated Pest Management Program for School Districts
Source: https://regulations.justia.com/states/texas/title-4/part-1/chapter-7/subchapter-h/

### 1a. Licence classes — **4 TAC §7.121, "Types and Requirements of Licenses"**
Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/4-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-7-121

| Subsection | Class | Distinguishing feature |
|---|---|---|
| (a) | **Business Licensee** | "Any person engaged in structural pest control for compensation must secure a business license in the business's operating name from the department for each business location," including branch offices. Each location needs its own designated responsible certified commercial applicator who does not serve in that role elsewhere. The licence reflects only categories in which at least one active certified applicator is employed. Must maintain the responsible applicator **and** current general liability insurance at all times. |
| (b) | **Responsible Certified Commercial Applicator** | A certified commercial applicator designated by the business licensee, "responsible for training and supervision of all pest control operations." May work as an ordinary certified applicator elsewhere but may be the *responsible* applicator for **only one** business-licence location. |
| (c) | **Certified Commercial Applicator** | Licensed in ≥1 category; "can perform pest control services, identifications, and control measures without direct supervision but under general supervision" of the responsible certified commercial applicator. Separate licence per employing business (branch offices need only one). Limited to licensed categories. |
| (d) | **Certified Noncommercial Applicator** | Employee of a governmental entity, apartment building, day care, hospital, nursing home, hotel/motel/lodge, warehouse, food-processing establishment, school, etc. "may perform pest control services, identifications, and control measures without direct supervision." |
| (e) | **Responsible Certified Noncommercial Applicator** | Designated by the noncommercial entity; "responsible for training, supervision, and records of all noncommercial apprentices and technicians." |
| (f) | **Technician** | Licensed in ≥1 category; "performs pest control services under the **direct supervision** of a certified commercial or noncommercial applicator." |
| (g) | **Apprentice** | Sales/service employee "registered by a structural pest control business or noncommercial entity to complete the required training for a technician license and has not yet passed a technician exam." Works only for the registering entity. |

**Key consumer-facing distinction:** business licence = the company; responsible certified applicator = the one named person accountable for training/supervision at that location; certified applicator = works unsupervised; technician = works under direct supervision; apprentice = unlicensed trainee.

### 1b. Categories — **4 TAC §7.124, "Structural License Categories"**
Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/4-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-7-124 (amended eff. 1/16/2023)

**Seven official categories:**

1. **Pest Control** — "The inspection or control of pests in and around structures or pest animals which may invade homes, restaurants, stores, and other buildings" (cockroaches, ants, fleas, flies, rodents, skunks, raccoons)
2. **Termite and Wood Destroying Insect Control** — "The inspection or control of termites, beetles, or other wood destroying insects and wood preservation" in structures, including termites in nearby trees
3. **Lawn and Ornamental** — "The inspection or control of pests or diseases of trees, shrubs, or other plantings"
4. **Weed Control** — "The inspection or control of weeds in right-of-ways, around homes, in a park or in and around structures"
5. **Structural Fumigation** — "The inspection or control of pests through fumigation of structures not primarily intended to contain food, feed, or grains"
6. **Commodity Fumigation** — "The inspection or control of pests through fumigation of processed commodities, raw agricultural commodities, or structures normally used to contain commodities"
7. **Wood Preservation** — "Pest control that involves the addition of preservatives to wood products," including re-treatment of power-line poles

**Note the exact official name is "Termite and Wood Destroying Insect Control"** — not "termite." Corroborated by Texas A&M AgriLife: https://schoolipm.tamu.edu/pesticide-applicator-information/how-to-obtain-a-license/

### 1c. Continuing education — **4 TAC §7.134, "Continuing Education Requirements for Certified Applicator"**
Sources: https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/4-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-7-134 and https://regulations.justia.com/states/texas/title-4/part-1/chapter-7/subchapter-h/division-2/section-7-134

Verbatim (Justia):
> **(a)** "Except as provided in subsections (f) and (j) of this section, as a requirement of annual license renewal, each certified applicator must certify that they have completed department-approved CEUs that cover the applicator's category(ies) of certification for the preceding calendar year."
>
> **(b)** "Each certified applicator is required to obtain two (2) CEUs in general training and one (1) unit in each category in which the applicator is certified."
>
> **(e)** "CEUs may only be obtained through an approved self-study course every other year. Self-study courses will not be accepted two (2) years in a row and will result in deficiency upon review."

Additional provisions (LII):
- (b) at least one general-training unit must cover **federal/state laws, pesticide safety, environmental protection, or integrated pest management**
- (c) "No approved course may be repeated for credit within the same calendar year."
- (d) instructors cannot claim CEU credit for courses they teach
- (f) first-year licensees and those newly certified in an added category are exempt for their initial period
- (g) certificates must be retained "for a period of two (2) calendar years"; providers must furnish certificates within 20 days
- (i) non-compliant applicators get 20 days to produce records; licences may be suspended in the deficient category
- (j) the Commissioner may grant hardship extensions

**Period = calendar year, annual renewal.** So a certified applicator in Pest Control + Termite + Lawn & Ornamental needs 2 general + 3 category = **5 CEUs per calendar year**.

⚠️ **CONFLICT FLAGGED:** Texas A&M AgriLife (https://schoolipm.tamu.edu/pesticide-applicator-information/how-to-obtain-a-license/) states "Only one CEU annually may come from self-study or internet courses." The rule text in §7.134(e) says self-study is available "every other year" and not two years running. **Follow the rule text; treat the AgriLife wording as an imprecise gloss.**

Source: https://regulations.justia.com/states/texas/title-4/part-1/chapter-7/subchapter-h/division-2/

**§7.126 — Qualification for Certified Commercial Applicator** (https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/4-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-7-126): must pass "the appropriate category examination" and "the general standards examination," plus one of: (1) "Verifiable employment in the pest control industry under the supervision of a licensed certified applicator for at least twelve (12) months out of the past twenty-four (24) months" **and** having held a technician's licence ≥6 months in that period; (2) documented prior out-of-state experience 12/24 months; (3) "A degree or certificate in an area of the biological sciences related to pest control from an accredited two (2) or four (4)-year college or university"; (4) previously held certified applicator licence; (5) hardship clause under §7.129.

**§7.132 — Apprentice** (https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/4-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-7-132): minimum age 16; application within 10 days of starting; card valid ~12 months; apprentice may not work without a licensed technician/certified applicator physically present until required training is documented, after which a certified applicator must be "physically present for personal instruction three (3) days a week." Training: 2 classroom hours in **each** of 10 general-standards subjects (= 20 hours), plus "forty (40) hours of verifiable on-the-job training and eight (8) hours of classroom training in each license category." Max 12 months as an apprentice, then all training must be repeated. Passing score on the technician exam: **70%**.

**§7.133 — Technician** (https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/4-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-7-133): adding a category requires "eight (8) hours of classroom hours training and forty (40) hours of on-the-job training." Renewal requires the certified applicator to certify "eight (8) hours of verifiable training in the calendar year preceding" renewal; up to "Two (2) hours … may be on-the-job training or hands-on-training"; "One (1) hour of credit for each department-approved CEU course completed." A certified applicator must be physically present at least one day weekly.

## 2. 4 TAC §7.146 — PEST CONTROL SIGN

Source (verbatim, cross-checked LII + Justia): https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/4-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-7-146 · https://regulations.justia.com/states/texas/title-4/part-1/chapter-7/subchapter-h/division-3/section-7-146/
**Effective date: January 16, 2023.** Official title: **"Pest Control Sign."**

> **(a)** A pest control sign must be provided by the licensee to a residential rental property owner or manager at least 48 hours prior to a planned indoor treatment at a residential rental property with five (5) or more rental units.
>
> **(b)** A pest control sign must be provided by the licensee to the employer or building manager at least 48 hours prior to a planned indoor treatment at a workplace.
>
> **(c)** A pest control sign must be provided by the licensee to the chief administrator, IPM Coordinator, or building manager at least 48 hours prior to a planned indoor treatment at a hospital, nursing home, hotel, motel, lodge, warehouse, food-processing establishment, school or educational institution, or day care center.
>
> **(d)** A person may not be considered in violation of this section if the space to be treated is vacant, unused, and unoccupied, or if extenuating circumstances require an emergency treatment.
>
> **(e)** Each pest control sign must be at least 8 1/2 inches by 11 inches in size and contain the required information with the first line in a minimum of 24-point type (one-fourth inch) and all remaining lines in a minimum of 12-point type (one-eighth inch). The addition of advertising and logos to the sign is permissible to the extent that such advertising does not interfere with the purpose of public notification of a pest control treatment. **A standard sign in Spanish is available from the department upon request.** The sign shall appear in a format approved by the department. The text and format of the sign is available on the Structural Pest Control Service website at: http://www.TexasAgriculture.gov/spcs, or by contacting the Texas Department of Agriculture, P.O. Box 12847, Austin, TX 78711-2847, (866) 918-4481.
>
> **(f)** In the space marked "For more information call or contact," the telephone number to obtain information on the pesticide(s) used must be listed, such as the contact number for the apartment manager, building manager, IPM Coordinator, or pest control operator.
>
> **(g)** In the space marked "phone number of hotline for pesticide information," the following wording must be used: National Pesticide Information Center 1-800-858-7378.
>
> **(h)** If a workplace has its own pesticide information center, the workplace center telephone number may be listed rather than the information in subsection (g) of this section.

### ⚠️ IMPORTANT CORRECTION TO YOUR FRAMING

**You asked me to "confirm 48 hours" for how long the sign must remain posted. That is NOT what the rule says, and I found no rule requiring a sign to *remain* posted for 48 hours.**

The 48 hours runs **forward**, not backward:
- §7.146 obliges the **licensee** to *hand the sign over* to the owner/manager ≥48 hours before treatment.
- **Posting** is the *unlicensed* party's duty, and it must occur ≥48 hours **before** each planned treatment — see §7.147(c)–(e) and §7.148 below.
- **NOT FOUND — do not write about this:** any minimum duration for which the sign must remain up after treatment.

**Who §7.146 applies to:** licensees (certified applicators/technicians) performing **indoor** treatments at the listed premises only. It does **not** apply to a single-family owner-occupied residence, or to a rental property with fewer than 5 units (those fall under §7.147(a)–(b), consumer-information-sheet-on-request only).

**Exemptions:** §7.146(d) — vacant/unused/unoccupied space, or emergency treatment. Also §7.147(g): the pre-notification requirements of §§7.146–7.148 "are waived if the customer and certified applicator sign a statement attesting to the fact that an emergency exists which requires immediate treatment… An emergency is defined as an imminent hazard to health," and treatment is limited to the localized area. The signed statement must be kept with the pest control use records.

### Companion rule — **4 TAC §7.148, "Responsibilities of Unlicensed Persons for Posting and Notification"** (verbatim, LII)

> **(a)** Owners or managers of residential rental properties with five (5) or more units must: (1) post a pest control sign at least 48 hours before the planned indoor treatment in an area of common access to residents; or (2) distribute the application information consistent with § 7.146(e) … to each unit planned to be treated and each unit adjacent to those planned to be treated or in an adjacent or area of common access at least 48 hours before the planned time of treatment; and (3) make the consumer information sheet available upon request.
>
> **(b)** Employers, building managers, IPM Coordinators, and chief administrators of workplaces, hospitals, nursing homes, hotels, motels, lodges, warehouses, food-processing establishments, school or educational institutions, and day care centers must post a pest control sign in an area of common access at least 48 hours prior to each planned indoor treatment and make a consumer information sheet available to any individual working or residing in the building upon the request of that individual.
>
> **(c)** Chief administrators or the IPM Coordinators of schools or educational institutions and day care centers must notify the parents or guardians of children attending the facility in writing that pesticides are periodically applied indoors and outdoors, and that information on the times and types of applications and prior notification is available upon request. Such notification must be made at the time of the students' registration…
>
> **(e)** A person may not be considered in violation of this section if a pest control sign is removed by an unauthorized person or if the space to be treated is vacant, unused, and unoccupied at the time of treatment.

**Statutory backing:** Tex. Occ. Code §1951.455(a)(1) requires notification "by posting the sign in an area of common access that the persons are likely to check on a regular basis **at least 48 hours before each planned treatment**." Source: https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._occ._code_section_1951.455 (related: §§1951.453 residential, 1951.454 workplace, 1951.457 outdoor treatments).

## 4. 4 TAC §7.173 — SUBTERRANEAN TERMITE PRE-CONSTRUCTION TREATMENTS

Sources: https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/4-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-7-173 · https://regulations.justia.com/states/texas/title-4/part-1/chapter-7/subchapter-h/division-5/section-7-173/ — effective 1/16/2023

⚠️ **Both mirrors returned summarized rather than fully verbatim text for this section.** Treat the following as accurate in substance but **verify exact wording before quoting.**

- **(a)** Scope carve-outs: subsections (b)–(f) do not apply to baits or baiting systems; (c)–(d) do not apply to wood-applied termiticide products.
- **(b)** All pre-construction termite pesticides must be registered with **both EPA and TDA**; "All pesticide liquid applications must be made by using the application rates and methods" per label instructions and precautionary statements.
- **(c)** **Full treatment** = continuous **horizontal and vertical** pesticide barriers throughout the structure. Final treatment must occur **within 30 days of landscaping completion or 1 year from construction completion, whichever comes first.** **Partial treatments** permitted only under specified conditions with a **signed statement** and an **amended diagram**, copies sent within **7 days**.
- **(d)** **Return visits required** — after slab pouring and after pier/beam placement — to complete the vertical barrier.
- **(e)** Primary wood framing treatment with **borate or equivalent** must follow full label instructions, be applied to exposed surfaces **before walls are enclosed**, to a minimum **2 feet** above slab contact (or 2-foot horizontal/vertical treatment above crawlspace/basement foundations), including around bath traps and plumbing penetrations.
- **(f)** **Pre-construction treatment notice** — for structures **other than single-family**, notice must be submitted to the department's system **4 to 24 hours before application**, only between **6:00 a.m. and 9:00 p.m.**, including address, treatment type, date/time, measurements and business details. **Cancellations require notice within 1 hour.**
- **(g)** **Records for commercial treatments:** measurements, termiticide amounts, mixing rates, **number of tanks and tank capacities**, and treatment **start/stop times** — **retained 2 years.**

**Signage / stickers:** §7.173 itself does **not** impose a sticker requirement — that sits in **§7.172(d)** (post-construction) and **§7.177** (WDI inspections). See §11 below. Consumer disclosure for pre-construction sits in **§7.174(b)(10)** — the "Proper Pre-Construction Subterranean Termite Treatments" guide must be included in the disclosure packet. TDA publishes it as **"Proper Preconstruction Termite Treatments -- Guide for Builders and Consumers."**

**NOT FOUND — do not write about this:** any pre-construction "certificate" of treatment issued to the homeowner as a distinct required document under §7.173. (The 2-year records and the §7.174 disclosure packet are what I could verify.)

## 5. WDI REPORTS

### The form

### Who may perform and sign — **4 TAC §7.175** (verbatim, LII; amended eff. 1/16/2023)
https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/4-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-7-175

> **(a)** Inspections for the purpose of issuing a WDIR must be conducted in a manner consistent with the procedures described in this section by **a licensed certified applicator or technician in the Termite and Wood Destroying Insect Control category.** The printed name and registration number or license number of any employees receiving training that are present for the inspection must be listed on the report. …Only for purposes of a Real Estate Transaction Inspection Report, as described in § 7.176…, there must be visible evidence of active infestation in the structure or visible evidence of a previous infestation in the structure with no evidence of prior treatment to recommend a corrective treatment. The inspection must be conducted so as to ensure examination of all visible accessible areas in or on a structure in accordance with accepted procedures. While such an examination may reveal wood destroying insects, there are instances when concealed infestations and/or damage may not be discovered. **Examinations of inaccessible or obstructed areas are not required.**

§7.175(b) lists department-recognized inaccessible/obstructed areas: inaccessible attics; hollow-wall interiors and spaces between a floor/porch deck and the ceiling or soffit below; porte cocheres, enclosed bay windows, buttresses and similar areas "to which there is no access without defacing or tearing out lumber, masonry, or finished work"; areas behind/beneath stoves, refrigerators, furniture, built-in cabinets, insulation and floor coverings; and "areas where storage conditions or locks make inspection impracticable."

§7.175(c) requires the report to describe the structure and include the address/location and a **diagram** showing approximate numerical perimeter measurements ("does not have to be to scale"), construction details needed for clarity, areas of **current** WDI activity, areas of **previous** WDI activity, and areas of **conditions conducive** to infestation — plus inaccessible/obstructed areas.

§7.175(d) mandatory inspection areas if accessible and unobstructed: plumbing (bathroom, kitchen, laundry, other e.g. hot tub); window and door frames and sills; baseboards, flooring, walls, ceilings; entrance steps and porches; exterior of slab or foundation wall; crawl spaces (support piers incl. stiff legs, floor joists, sub floors, sill plates, foundation wall); fireplace; weep holes.

§7.175(e) conditions that must be reported: WDI or signs of current active infestation; termite tubes or frass; exit holes or frass from other WDI; evidence of previous treatment or infestation; **conditions conducive** — "(A) a structure with wood to ground contact; (B) formboards left in place; (C) excessive moisture; (D) wood debris under or around structure; (E) footing too low or soil line too high; (F) insufficient clearance in crawl space; (G) expansion joints or cracks in slab; and (H) decks"; and infestation of other wood destroying insects.

## 8. TEXAS PROPERTY CODE §92.052 AND RELATED

### §92.052 — Landlord's Duty to Repair or Remedy
**Verified verbatim, two independent mirrors in exact agreement:**
https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/property-code/title-8/chapter-92/subchapter-b/section-92-052/ · https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._prop._code_section_92.052

> **Sec. 92.052. LANDLORD'S DUTY TO REPAIR OR REMEDY.**
>
> **(a)** A landlord shall make a diligent effort to repair or remedy a condition if:
> (1) the tenant specifies the condition in a notice to the person to whom or to the place where rent is normally paid;
> (2) the tenant is not delinquent in the payment of rent at the time notice is given; and
> (3) the condition:
> (A) **materially affects the physical health or safety of an ordinary tenant**; or
> (B) arises from the landlord's failure to provide and maintain in good operating condition a device to supply hot water of a minimum temperature of 120 degrees Fahrenheit.
>
> **(b)** Unless the condition was caused by normal wear and tear, the landlord does not have a duty during the lease term or a renewal or extension to repair or remedy a condition caused by:
> (1) the tenant; (2) a lawful occupant in the tenant's dwelling; (3) a member of the tenant's family; or (4) a guest or invitee of the tenant.
>
> **(c)** This subchapter does not require the landlord:
> (1) to furnish utilities from a utility company if as a practical matter the utility lines of the company are not reasonably available; or
> (2) to furnish security guards.
>
> **(d)** The tenant's notice under Subsection (a) must be in writing only if the tenant's lease is in writing and requires written notice.

### Does any Texas statute specifically address pest infestation in rental property?

**NO — and this is the single most important precision point in this whole report.**

I enumerated every section of Property Code Chapter 92, Subchapter B (Repair or Closing of Leasehold): §92.051 Application · §92.052 Landlord's Duty to Repair or Remedy · §92.053 Burden of Proof · §92.054 Casualty Loss · §92.055 Closing the Rental Premises · §92.056 Landlord Liability and Tenant Remedies; Notice and Time for Repair · §92.0561 Tenant's Repair and Deduct Remedies · §92.0562 Landlord Affidavit for Delay · §92.0563 Tenant's Judicial Remedies · §92.058 Landlord Remedy for Tenant Violation · §92.060 Agents for Delivery of Notice · §92.061 Effect on Other Rights · §92.062 Lease Term After Natural Disaster.
Source: https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/property-code/title-8/chapter-92/subchapter-b/

**None of them mention pests, insects, vermin, rodents, bed bugs, or infestation.** Chapter 92's subchapters are: General Provisions · Repair or Closing of Leasehold · Security Deposits · Security Devices · Disclosure of Ownership and Management · Smoke Alarms and Fire Extinguishers · Utility Cutoff · Retaliation · Rental Application. There is no pest subchapter.

**The correct, defensible statement for a compliance page:** *Texas has no statute expressly requiring landlords to control pests. A pest infestation reaches the landlord's repair duty only if it is a condition that "materially affects the physical health or safety of an ordinary tenant" under Property Code §92.052(a)(3)(A) — a fact question — and only if the tenant follows the §92.052/§92.056 notice procedure and is current on rent.* Note also §92.052(b): the landlord has no duty for a condition **caused by** the tenant, a lawful occupant, a family member, or a guest/invitee, unless it was normal wear and tear.

⚠️ **Do not repeat the widely-circulated claim (found on numerous pest-control company blogs) that Texas law "requires landlords to provide pest control."** No statute I could locate says that.

### Tenant's notice procedure — §92.056
**Partially verbatim only.** Justia refused to reproduce it; texas.public.law and FindLaw both returned condensed renderings. Cross-checked and consistent across those two:
https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._prop._code_section_92.056 · https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/property-code/prop-sect-92-056/

The landlord is liable to the tenant only if all six of §92.056(b) are met: (1) the tenant gave notice to repair at the place rent is normally paid; (2) **the condition materially affects the physical health or safety of an ordinary tenant**; (3) after a reasonable time the tenant gave a **subsequent written notice**, or the original notice was sent by **certified mail / trackable mail**; (4) the landlord has had a reasonable time to repair after receiving notice; (5) the landlord has not made a diligent effort to repair; (6) the tenant was not delinquent in rent when notice was given.

**Verbatim (c):** *"For purposes of Subsection (b)(4) or (5), a landlord is considered to have received the tenant's notice when the landlord or the landlord's agent or employee has actually received the notice or when the United States Postal Service has attempted to deliver the notice to the landlord."*

**(d)** There is a **rebuttable presumption that seven days is a reasonable time** to repair, rebuttable by the date of notice, the severity and nature of the condition, and the availability of materials, labor and utilities.
**(e)** Remedies if the landlord is liable: terminate the lease; have the condition repaired and deduct per §92.0561; deduct the cost from rent without court action per §92.0561; or obtain judicial remedies per §92.0563.
**(f)** On termination the tenant gets a pro rata rent refund and security deposit return, but forfeits the repair-and-deduct and certain judicial remedies.
**(g)** The lease must state the tenant's remedies under §92.056 and §92.0561 **in underlined or bold print.**

## 9. HEALTH & SAFETY CODE CHAPTERS 341, 342, 343

### Direct answer to your two questions
- **Which chapter gives MUNICIPALITIES the weed abatement power?** → **Chapter 342** (specifically §342.004, with the procedure in §§342.006–342.008).
- **Which gives COUNTIES it?** → **Chapter 343** (§343.011(c)(4) defines the nuisance; §§343.021–343.022 give the abatement authority and procedure) — and it applies **only to the unincorporated area of a county** (§343.011(a)).

### Chapter 341 — statewide public health nuisance definitions
**§341.011, "Nuisance"** (verbatim, https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._health_and_safety_code_section_341.011, cross-checked against https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/health-and-safety-code/health-safety-sect-341-011/) — **"Each of the following is a public health nuisance:"**

> (1) "a condition or place that is a breeding place for flies and that is in a populous area"
> (2) "spoiled or diseased meats intended for human consumption"
> (3) "a restaurant, food market, bakery, other place of business, or vehicle in which food is prepared, packed, stored, transported, sold, or served to the public and that is not constantly maintained in a sanitary condition"
> (4) "a place, condition, or building controlled or operated by a state or local government agency that is not maintained in a sanitary condition"
> (5) "sewage, human excreta, wastewater, garbage, or other organic wastes deposited, stored, discharged, or exposed in such a way as to be a potential instrument or medium in disease transmission"
> (6) "a vehicle or container that is used to transport garbage, human excreta, or other organic material and that is defective and allows leakage or spilling of contents"
> (7) **"a collection of water in which mosquitoes are breeding in the limits of a municipality or a collection of water that is a breeding area for mosquitoes that can transmit diseases"**
> (8) "a condition that may be proven to injuriously affect the public health" [from bone boiling, fat rendering, or similar operations]
> (9) **"a place or condition harboring rats in a populous area"**
> (10) **"the presence of ectoparasites, including bedbugs, lice, and mites, suspected to be disease carriers in a place in which sleeping accommodations are offered to the public"**
> (11) "the maintenance of an open surface privy or an overflowing septic tank so that the contents may be accessible to flies"
> (12) "an object, place, or condition that is a possible and probable medium of disease transmission to or between humans"

**Items (7), (9) and (10) are the pest-relevant ones — note (10) covers bedbugs in hotels/motels/public sleeping accommodations. This is the closest thing Texas has to a bedbug statute.**

> **(a)** "A person shall abate a public health nuisance existing in or on a place the person possesses as soon as the person knows that the nuisance exists."
> **(b)** "A local health authority who receives information and proof that a public health nuisance exists in the local health authority's jurisdiction shall issue a written notice ordering the abatement of the nuisance to any person responsible for the nuisance." [copy to municipal/county/district attorney]
> **(c)** "The notice must specify the nature of the public health nuisance and designate a reasonable time within which the nuisance must be abated."
> **(d)** If not abated in time, the health authority notifies the prosecuting attorney, who must immediately commence abatement proceedings or request the attorney general to initiate/assist.

**What Ch. 341 authorises regarding your four items:** stagnant water → yes, via §341.011(7) (mosquito-breeding water collections). Vermin → yes, via (9) rats and (10) ectoparasites/bedbugs. Rubbish → indirectly, via (5) and (12). Weeds → **no**; Chapter 341 does not mention weeds.

## 10. TEXAS LOCAL GOVERNMENT CODE §217.042

Sources: https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/local-government-code/loc-gov-t-sect-217-042/ (current through Jan 1, 2026) and https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/2009/local-government-code/title-7-regulation-of-land-use-structures-businesses-and-related-activities/chapter-217-municipal-regulation-of-nuisances-and-disorderly-conduct

**Chapter 217 = "Municipal Regulation of Nuisances and Disorderly Conduct."**

### ⚠️ CORRECTION TO YOUR FRAMING
You described §217.042 as "municipal nuisance abatement authority" generally. **It is narrower than that: §217.042 sits in Subchapter C — REGULATION BY HOME-RULE MUNICIPALITY.** So "the municipality" in the text means a **home-rule municipality**, not any Texas city. Do not present it as a blanket grant to all municipalities.

**Sec. 217.042. NUISANCE.**
> **(a)** [A home-rule municipality] "may define and prohibit any nuisance within the limits of the municipality and within 5,000 feet outside the limits."
> **(b)** "The municipality may enforce all ordinances necessary to prevent and summarily abate and remove a nuisance."
> **(c)** "The municipality may not define and prohibit as a nuisance the sale of fireworks or similar materials outside the limits of the municipality."

Legislative source: Acts 1987, 70th Leg., ch. 149, Sec. 1, eff. Sept. 1, 1987 (subsection (c) added later).

⚠️ Subsections (a) and (b) are confirmed by two independent sources. **Subsection (c) appears only in the current FindLaw version** (the 2009 Justia snapshot has only (a) and (b)) — consistent with a later amendment, but verify (c)'s presence and exact text before publishing.

**Practical note for a pest-control compliance page:** §217.042 is the "any nuisance" grant — it lets a home-rule city define, e.g., harborage conditions or standing water as a nuisance by ordinance and abate summarily, including up to 5,000 feet outside the city limits. It complements, and does not replace, H&S Code §342.004.

### Home-solicitation cancellation right
TDA's Consumer Information Sheet says consumers who contract via home solicitation "have the right to cancel the contract within **72 hours**."

⚠️ **CONFLICT FLAGGED.** The governing statute — **Tex. Bus. & Com. Code §601.052** (https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._bus._and_com._code_section_601.052) — requires the merchant to give a receipt/contract copy at execution, in the language of the oral sales presentation, with the date and the merchant's name and address, and in **boldfaced type of at least 10 points**, in immediate proximity to the signature space (or on the front page of a receipt):

> **"YOU, THE BUYER, MAY CANCEL THIS TRANSACTION AT ANY TIME PRIOR TO MIDNIGHT OF THE THIRD BUSINESS DAY AFTER THE DATE OF THIS TRANSACTION."**

**"Third business day" ≠ "72 hours."** Over a weekend or holiday the statutory period is materially longer. **Publish the statutory language, not TDA's 72-hour shorthand.**
